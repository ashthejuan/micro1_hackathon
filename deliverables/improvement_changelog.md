### Main contribution

The **deterministic citation guard** (`agents.py:98` `_bad_refs` + `agents.py:269` single retry) on top of the **SQLite set-check verifier** (`store.py:146`). Without it, the typed schemas alone hallucinate real-looking `E99`; with it, `v=0.0 → 1.0`. Everything else (fan-out causality, consult-only memory, human gate, replay) preserves that gain while adding auditability, recurrence awareness, and reproducibility.

### Remaining failure mode & hot take

**Failure mode — citation integrity ≠ semantic truth.** The verifier confirms `E5 ∈ valid_ids`, not that `E5` *supports* the claim. A claim "DB CPU spike [E5] caused outage" would still pass `backed=True` because `E5` exists, even though `E5` is the red herring. The report is explicit about this (`context/PRD.md:554`): verification is paired with **red_herring rejection** (label-vs-truth) and **completeness** to cover the gap — and the trajectory `verifier.json:verifier_math` makes the set-check auditable so a human can catch the semantic mismatch at the gate. `INC-004` `verifier.json` shows `claimed_refs:[E1,E4]` vs `valid_ids:[E1..E8]` and `backed:true` alongside `contradicting_evidence:[E5]` in `analyze.json`.

**Hot take:** *A Pydantic-typed output + a SQLite set-check mattered more than model choice or LLM-based contradiction detection for trustworthy, reproducible incident postmortems — with the honest caveat that verification must be paired with an explicit red-herring metric and a human gate, or the scores are theatre.* That lesson generalizes: when the agent must be auditable, constrain the LLM with schemas + a deterministic oracle at the boundary, don't ask the LLM to judge itself.
